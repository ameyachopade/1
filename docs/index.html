Abstract

This research addresses the challenge of identifying and prioritizing decaying organic search content across enterprise domains before traffic collapse occurs. Using a multi-client dataset of 30,000 anonymized web pages across 32 clients, we framed organic decay detection as a risk scoring and ranking task evaluated at Precision@K. We engineered a transparent heuristic baseline combining historical search visibility, content staleness, and ranking vulnerability, and compared it against learned models (Logistic Regression, Decision Tree, and Random Forest) using a strict Grouped Client-Holdout split. Standardized Logistic Regression achieved a Precision@20 of 80.0% and Precision@50 of 80.0% on completely unseen clients—a substantial +28.9 percentage point lift over the test set base rate of 51.1% and beating the rule baseline's 50.0% Precision@20. The resulting action engine outputs ranked content refresh recommendations with human-interpretable reason codes to direct editorial bandwidth toward the highest-leverage decay opportunities.

01 Introduction & Problem Statement

Content decay—the gradual loss of organic search impressions and clicks over time—is the single largest driver of organic traffic erosion on enterprise websites. In organic search, editorial and growth teams face an acute operational bottleneck: while thousands of pages may be several months old, only a fraction require immediate editorial intervention. Reviewing every page manually is cost-prohibitive, while blind scheduled refreshes waste valuable editorial bandwidth on stable evergreen content or low-opportunity pages.

This work builds an automated, predictive decision-support system that ranks candidate pages by predicted organic decline risk and attaches human-readable reason codes (stale_striking_low_ctr, stale_striking_distance) and suggested action labels (review_snippet_and_ctr, content_refresh). By prioritizing intervention before pages slip from Page 1 into organic obscurity, editorial teams can focus their limited hours on defending high-value revenue keywords.

02 Data & Public Safety Hygiene

We utilized the FlyRank multi-client starter release comprising 30,000 anonymized web content items across 32 client organizations. Each row represents a single content page aggregated across a trailing 90-day historical window.

Leakage Prevention & Exclusion Controls:

  • Strict Forward-Window Exclusion: All forward comparison metrics (impressions_last_30d, clicks_last_30d, sessions_last_30d, impressions_prev_30d, clicks_prev_30d, sessions_prev_30d) were completely excluded from features to eliminate future-window lookahead.
  • Target Derivative Exclusion: Outcome labels (trend_direction, trend_pct) were withheld from training, as the target is_declining_label is derived directly from them.
  • ID Reservation: Pseudonymous IDs (content_id, client_id) were used strictly for joins and grouped validation splits, never as model features.

Public-Safety Guarantee: All client names, domain URLs, user IDs, and raw search queries are fully anonymized. No proprietary identifiers or credentials exist anywhere in the code or data.

03 Methodology & Validation Design

To prevent client identity leakage (where models memorize client-specific domain authority or tracking baselines), we partitioned the dataset using a strict Grouped Client-Holdout Split (GroupShuffleSplit on client_id, 80% train / 20% test).

Partition Items (Rows) Clients Base Declining Rate Role
Training Set 23,837 25 clients 55.0% Model fitting and parameter estimation
Held-Out Test Set 6,163 7 clients 51.1% Unseen client out-of-domain evaluation

We benchmarked three model families against a transparent heuristic baseline:

  1. Week 4 Heuristic Baseline: Deterministic rule: $\ln(1 + \text{impressions\_90d}) \times (0.50 \cdot \mathbb{I}_{\text{stale}} + 0.30 \cdot \mathbb{I}_{\text{striking}} + 0.20 \cdot \mathbb{I}_{\text{low\_ctr}})$.
  2. Logistic Regression: Standardized linear model producing calibrated probability scores for threshold sorting.
  3. Decision Tree (depth=5): Depth-constrained tree evaluating non-linear threshold splits without overfitting.
  4. Random Forest: 100-tree ensemble with bagged subspace sampling.

05 Feature Interpretation & Error Analysis

Standardized Logistic Regression weights reveal the primary drivers of organic traffic decay:

  • log_impressions_90d (+1.347): High impression volume is the strongest positive decay predictor. Highly visible pages experience the greatest competitive displacement pressure and Google SERP layout changes.
  • log_clicks_90d (-0.706): Active click volume is protective; continuous click engagement sustains search ranking positions.
  • content_age_days (-0.373): Older content that survived historical algorithm updates displays evergreen resilience.
  • avg_position (-0.241): Pages at the top of the SERP have the greatest downside room to slip.

Error Breakdown with a Skeptic's Eye:

False Positives (High Predicted Decay, Actually Stable): Newly indexed pages (age ~96d, e.g. content_7be5f150dc65, prob=94.7%) had 0% CTR because impressions had just begun accruing. The model mistakenly interpreted low initial click volume as decay.

False Negatives (Low Predicted Decay, Actually Declined): Flagship articles (>500k impressions, e.g. content_5fe46e04994d, prob=0.8%) possessed rock-solid historical metrics, yet experienced macro SERP volatility (such as AI Overview introductions) that historical aggregates cannot anticipate.

06 Limitations & Honest Framing

We explicitly state what this model cannot claim:

  • No Causal Refresh Guarantees: The model predicts observational organic search decline; it does not prove that refreshing a page causally recovers traffic without a randomized intervention experiment.
  • Not a Reverse-Engineered Ranking Algorithm: Observed correlations reflect empirical behavior across a trailing 90-day snapshot, not Google's internal ranking heuristics.
  • SERP Feature Confounders: Pages with near-zero CTR in top 5 positions often reflect image pack or snippet syndication where zero clicks is standard.
